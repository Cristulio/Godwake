# Rogue: weak class, or under-played by the bot? — sim findings (2026-06-01)

Branch `feat/rogue-ai-sim`. Settles the question raised by the Barbarian power
read (#271), which showed **Rogue weakest at the floor**. The mirror case is the
caster-AI lane (#211), where the bot — not the wizard — was the bottleneck, and a
better policy lifted A0 clear 50→87.5%. Does the same hold for Rogue?

## TL;DR verdict

**The Rogue's in-sim weakness is GENUINE class-floor fragility, NOT bot
under-play.** The opposite of the wizard case. The bot already fires Sneak Attack
competently (2.39×/combat, 0.45×/turn) and plays its scarce Cunning Action / Hide
charge close to optimally for the bare-soul early game. Two independent "play it
better" levers were measured and **both made the Rogue worse**, not better:

| Variant | Avg depth (rooms) | Avg final lvl | Sneak/combat | Sneak/turn | Hide/combat |
|--------|------------------:|--------------:|-------------:|-----------:|------------:|
| **Shipped policy (baseline)** | **9.9** | **2.42** | **2.39** | **0.45** | **1.75** |
| Bank Hide off the free opening strike | 7.8 | 2.06 | 2.25 | 0.45 | 1.20 |
| Wield the dagger (auto-Sneak every swing) | 5.5 | 1.63 | 2.61 | 0.53 | 0.00 |

(`SOULS_PER_CLASS=50 MAX_LIVES=50 ARCHETYPE=balanced`, same seed schedule across
all three.)

So Sneak Attack is not the lever. The wall is **survival at the bare-soul floor**:
the Rogue dies in Chapter 1 at roughly triple the rate of any other class, at mean
level ~2.4, before it ever reaches the L3 power spike (Thief's second Cunning
Action charge + 2d6 Sneak) or L5 Uncanny Dodge. This matches the prior
`rogue-meta-journey-sim2` read: the kit clears the full ladder once it gets
rolling, but the bare-soul early game is the wall.

## How the Rogue's Sneak Attack actually works (the audit)

Sneak Attack fires once per turn when the strike "has the angle". The engine
(`playerAttack.ts`) grants that on ANY of:

- **the opening strike** of each combat (`!state.playerHasAttacked`) — always, no setup;
- a **bloodied** mark (target ≤ ½ HP);
- the swing rolled with **advantage** (Hide via Cunning Action, Reckless, a first-attack blessing);
- a **dagger** in hand (the Rogue's quick-blade synergy — fires every swing);
- the Swashbuckler / Assassin subclass loosened triggers.

The default Rogue wields a **rapier** (dagger sits unused in inventory), and the
sim's Thief takes the first subclass — so in practice Sneak rides the opening
strike, bloodied marks, and the Hide charge.

`actionPolicy.ts` opens with Cunning Action: Hide whenever the focus target is
above half HP and a charge is free, then attacks the lowest-HP target. The
instrumentation confirms it lands **2.39 Sneak Attacks per combat** — the
mechanic is firing, not dormant.

## Why the "obvious" policy fix backfires

The tempting optimization: the opening strike already Sneaks for free, so don't
"waste" the scarce Hide charge there — bank it for a later, healthy-target turn
that has no other trigger. Implemented (skip Hide when `!playerHasAttacked`, on a
dagger, on a bloodied mark, or after Sneak already fired this turn), it **dropped
depth 9.9 → 7.8 and level 2.42 → 2.06**, with Sneak/turn unchanged at 0.45.

The reason is instructive: the opening Hide was never about the Sneak *trigger* —
it was about the **to-hit advantage**. A bare-soul Rogue (d8 HP, leather, ~9–10
HP at L1) has to land its burst before the Chapter-1 swarm drops it. Advantage on
the opening strike protects exactly that. Banking the charge for a turn the Rogue
often does not survive to reach trades a real survival edge for a theoretical one.

The dagger variant is even more decisive: guaranteed Sneak *every* swing lifted
Sneak/turn to 0.53, but **depth collapsed to 5.5** — losing the rapier's larger
weapon die and the Hide-advantage to-hit cost far more than the extra Sneak turns
returned. **More Sneak Attacks did not mean more survival.**

## Where the Rogue actually dies

Deaths by chapter (baseline, 50 souls × up to 50 lives):

- **Rogue — ch1: 1765 · ch2: 618 · ch3: 8 · ch4: 10 · ch5: 14 · ch6: 22.**
- For contrast: ranger ch1 586, wizard ch1 581, fighter ch1 535, barbarian ch1 410.

The Rogue's Chapter-1 deaths are ~3× the pack. Top kill-rooms are ordinary early
encounters: skeleton+bone-stalker, skeleton+stirge, goblin+kobold, famished-ghast.
Nothing exotic — it is the bare-soul L1–L2 floor, repeatedly. Depth 9.9 vs the
pack (fighter 51.4, ranger 32.8, barbarian 26.0, wizard 15.2) makes the Rogue the
shallowest class by a wide margin, dominated entirely by these early deaths.

## Tuning direction (no numbers — the user decides)

This is a balance question, not a policy one — no balance changes were made in
this lane. If the Rogue's floor is to be raised, the lever is **bare-soul L1–L2
survivability**, not Sneak frequency (already saturated). Two coherent directions:

- **Shore up the early hull** — the d8/leather bare soul has no clutch heal (unlike
  the Fighter's per-combat Second Wind) and no defensive tool until L5 Uncanny
  Dodge. An early survivability lever (HP, AC, or an earlier defensive Cunning
  Action option the bot would actually use) would attack the wall directly.
- **Pull the power spike earlier** — the Rogue's identity is back-loaded (Thief's
  second charge + 2d6 Sneak at L3, Uncanny Dodge at L5), but it dies at ~L2.4.
  Moving part of that forward would let the kit come online before the floor kills it.

Either is the user's call. The data only says: the bot is not the bottleneck here.

## What changed in this lane

- **`scripts/sim-class-viability.ts`** — added Sneak Attack / Hide proc
  instrumentation (Sneak/combat, Sneak/turn, Hide/combat), symmetric with the
  existing Rage / Reckless / Hunter's-Mark counters. This is the competence
  check; it stays in the sim.
- **No `actionPolicy.ts` change shipped.** The two candidate improvements were
  measured and both regressed the floor, so the existing policy stands.
